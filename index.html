Your Guide to This Report
Read this once. Everything in the report will make more sense.

What is this? This report shows how the human brain responds to video advertising — second by second. We used TRIBE v2, an AI model trained on thousands of real fMRI brain scans (MRI recordings of people watching content), to predict how a viewer's brain would activate while watching each film. You don't need anyone in a scanner. You just need the video.

What did we test? 10 IPL campaign films — 5 from 2025 and 5 from 2026 — were run through the TRIBE v2 model. The output is a per-second brain activation signal across 7 neural networks for each film. From that signal, we calculate 5 brand metrics that tell you whether a film will be noticed, felt, and remembered.

Why does this matter? Standard research (recall surveys, focus groups) measures what people say they felt — after the fact. Brain signals measure what they actually felt — in the moment. The two are often very different. This report closes that gap.

The 7 Brain Networks (Yeo-7 Atlas)

The brain is divided into 7 functional networks. Each one activates for different types of content. TRIBE maps all 7 per second.

👁 Visual
Processes what the eye sees — motion, colour, faces on screen. High = visually stimulating.
🏃 Somotor
Body and movement response — physicality, action, energy. High = kinetic, active content.
🎯 DAN
Directed Attention Network — focused concentration on a specific thing. High = content demands and gets focus.
⚡ VAN
Ventral Attention Network — alertness, surprise, novelty. High = something unexpected just happened.
❤️ Limbic
Emotion and memory — warmth, feeling, desire, connection. The most important network for brand recall.
💡 FPN
Fronto-Parietal Network — thinking, planning, meaning-making. High = message is being understood and processed.
💭 DMN
Default Mode Network — daydreaming, mind-wandering. We want this low. High DMN = the viewer has mentally checked out.
⚡ Engagement
A composite score: 0.25×Visual + 0.25×DAN + 0.20×VAN + 0.15×Limbic + 0.15×FPN − 0.05×|DMN−0.3|. The headline brain activation number.
The Brain Score — How It's Calculated

Formula: Brain Score = (Global × 25% + Temporal × 45% + HPE × 30%) × Hook Multiplier × 100

Three components feed into every Brain Score:

GLOBAL — 25%
"What kind of content is this overall?" — Mean engagement quality, emotional depth, attention level, and how little of the film was dead zone. A baseline quality score for the whole piece.
TEMPORAL — 45% (highest weight)
"How does the brain move through the film?" — Momentum, emotional power, absorption, % of time in flow state. The journey matters more than the average. A film that builds scores higher than one that flatlines.
HPE — 30%
"Did the three critical moments land?" — Hook (opening), Peak (best single moment), Ending (close). A film must work at all three narrative anchors, not just one.
⚡ Hook Multiplier — the penalty gate If the opening is weak, the entire Brain Score gets cut — regardless of how good the rest of the film is. A hook multiplier of 0.6 means a 40% penalty on the final score. This is why two films with similar Engagement can have very different Brain Scores — the one with the weaker opening gets penalised heavily.

Example: GOAT vs YOAT scores 63.5 because Global=78%, Temporal=54%, HPE=66%, Hook Multiplier=1.0 (strong open). Virat 18 scores only 29.1 despite similar content — its Hook Multiplier was 0.6, cutting the entire score by 40%.

The 5 Brand Metrics — Plain English
🎯
Hook
First impression · The skip window
What:
How strongly the brain activates in the opening of the film — before a viewer can skip or scroll away.
How:
Mean brain Engagement across the first 20% of the film's duration. A 30s film = first 6 seconds. A 60s film = first 12 seconds. This is more accurate than a fixed 5-second window because shorter and longer films earn attention differently.
Good:
Above 40% is a strong hook. Below 25% is a skip risk on paid social.
Why:
The opening window determines whether a viewer stays or leaves. Every second of engagement you lose here costs you reach.
💡
Engagement
Sustained attention · Did they stay?
What:
Average brain activation across the entire film. This is the single most comparable number across all films.
How:
Mean of the Engagement composite (formula above) across all seconds of the film.
Good:
Above 50% is solid. Above 55% is strong. Below 45% the brain is frequently drifting.
Why:
Every second of engagement is a second your message was received. Low engagement = expensive wasted seconds.
❤️
Emotion
Limbic activation · Did it make them feel?
What:
How emotionally activated the brain was — how warm, connected, or moved the viewer felt.
How:
Two numbers: Mean Emotion % (average Limbic activation — comparable across all films) + Peak Emotion Seconds (how many seconds the Limbic network crossed the 75% threshold — high-intensity emotional moments).
Good:
Mean above 55%. Peak Secs above 15s for a 30–60s film is meaningful.
Why:
Emotion drives memory and brand association. You remember ads that made you feel something. High Limbic activation = higher recall and purchase consideration.
📈
Arc Score
Brand readiness · Does it build to a close?
What:
Whether the film builds emotionally toward the end — where your brand appears.
How:
√(Momentum × Ending Score). Momentum measures whether brain activation trends upward through the second half. Ending measures brain activation in the final 10% of the film. Arc takes the geometric mean — both halves must be strong for a high score.
Good:
Above 55 is a crescendo film. Below 40 means the emotional peak came too early.
Why:
Your brand closes the film. If the brain is cool at that moment, the brand message doesn't land. A high Arc Score = the brain is most primed exactly when your brand appears. Think of it like a concert that saves the biggest song for the finale.
🚀
Momentum
Story trajectory · Does energy build or fade?
What:
Whether the film's brain activation is rising or falling as the film progresses.
How:
Compares brain engagement in the second half vs the first half of the film. A score above 50% means the second half is stronger — a crescendo. Below 50% means it peaked early and fades — a firecracker.
Good:
Above 50% = the film builds. Above 65% = strong crescendo arc.
Why:
High-momentum films create anticipation. The viewer leans in as it progresses. Low-momentum films feel front-loaded — they peak early and coast, leaving the brand close on a cooling brain. Momentum is one half of the Arc Score.

🔎 Campaign Verdict — Pros & Cons
🟣 IPL 2026 — Designed to Start Strong
✅
Wins the opening window. Hook of 41.5% is +15.3pts above IPL25. Best film: Chote Kadam (54.8%). On social and pre-roll where viewers skip in 3–5 seconds, IPL26 is the clear winner.
✅
Higher mean engagement. 52.7% vs 48.1% — brains stay active across the runtime. Best: Ab Ek Hoke (54.7%).
⚠️
Weak story arc. Arc score 40.5 vs IPL25's 62.4. Films peak in the first half — the brand close lands when brains are cooling. Emotion is front-loaded, not a crescendo.
⚠️
Low brand-close readiness. Ending score avg 42.0% vs IPL25's 60.1%. The final seconds — when your brand appears — are the weakest part of IPL26 films neurally.
🟠 IPL 2025 — Built to Be Remembered
✅
Strongest story arc. Arc score 62.4 — emotion builds toward the brand reveal. Best film: MI Champions (arc 79.9). Drives brand recall and consideration.
✅
Emotion lands at the right moment. Momentum 65.9% + Ending 60.1% means the brain is most primed exactly when the brand closes. The signature of high-recall creative.
⚠️
Slow burn opening. Hook 26.2% — the lowest of both campaigns. IPL25 films need a viewer already willing to commit. Higher skip risk on fast-scroll environments.
⚠️
High variance across films. One standout film (MI Champions) pulls the average up. Not all films deliver the arc equally well.
🏆 Strategic verdict: Use IPL26 for top-of-funnel — paid social, pre-roll, short-format. The hook wins the scroll. Use IPL25 for mid-to-bottom funnel — connected TV, YouTube full-view, cinema. The arc wins the memory.

🎯 The ideal 2027 brief: IPL26's opening hook (41.5%) + IPL25's arc (62.4) in a single film. Neither campaign has cracked this yet — it's the creative opportunity for next year.
